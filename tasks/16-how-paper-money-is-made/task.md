# Task: Deep Research — How Paper Money Is Made and Enters Circulation

Write the result to `output.md` in this folder.

## Objective
Produce a deep-research report explaining, end-to-end, how paper money (banknotes/currency) comes into existence and reaches the public: who decides how much to create, how it's physically designed and printed, and the mechanism by which newly printed currency actually enters circulation (as opposed to just sitting in a vault). This is a research task, not an opinion piece — every factual claim must be accurate and, where possible, sourced. Use the United States (Federal Reserve / Bureau of Engraving and Printing) as the primary worked example, but briefly note how other major central banks (e.g., ECB, Bank of England, Bank of Japan) differ where relevant.

## Scope
At minimum, address:
- **Who decides how much currency to produce** — the roles of the central bank (e.g., Federal Reserve Board) versus the government printer (e.g., U.S. Bureau of Engraving and Printing / BEP), and how print orders are determined (replacing worn-out notes, meeting demand growth, etc.).
- **Design & security features** — how a banknote design is created and approved, and the major anti-counterfeiting features used in modern notes (watermarks, security threads, color-shifting ink, microprinting, etc.) and why they exist.
- **The physical printing process** — the paper/substrate (cotton-linen blend vs. polymer notes used by some other countries), the printing techniques (e.g., intaglio, offset, letterpress), and the production steps from blank sheet to finished note.
- **From printer to circulation — the actual mechanism** — this is the core of the question: newly printed currency is not "released" directly to the public. Explain the real pathway: printer → central bank vaults → commercial/regional banks ordering cash to meet customer demand → ATMs/bank branches → the public. Clarify that new physical currency creation is distinct from "money creation" in the economic sense (which mostly happens via bank lending / central bank operations, not printing presses).
- **Currency lifecycle** — how worn/damaged notes are removed from circulation and destroyed, and replaced.
- **Oversight & legal authority** — the legal basis for who is authorized to issue currency (e.g., in the U.S., the Federal Reserve Act) and why counterfeiting outside this process is illegal.

## Required structure of `output.md`
1. **Title + scope line** stating the coverage and the date the research was performed.
2. **TL;DR** — 4-6 bullet points giving the short version of the full pathway from "decision to print" to "cash in your wallet."
3. **The pathway, step by step** — a numbered or diagram-style walkthrough (e.g., Federal Reserve Board sets order → BEP designs & prints → shipped to Federal Reserve Banks → commercial banks buy/order cash to meet customer demand → ATMs/tellers → public), with each step explained in a short paragraph.
4. **Design and security features** — dedicated section on how a note is designed and the anti-counterfeiting measures used.
5. **Printing process** — dedicated section on the physical production (materials, presses, techniques).
6. **Money creation vs. money printing** — a clarifying section distinguishing physical currency printing (a small, mechanical part of the money supply) from broader monetary/credit creation, so the report doesn't leave the misleading impression that printing presses are the primary source of the money supply.
7. **Comparative note** — brief comparison of how 1-2 other major central banks/mints handle the same pathway (e.g., polymer notes and the Bank of England, or the Eurosystem's multiple national central banks).
8. **Sources** — consolidated list of references used (official central bank/government sources preferred), each with enough detail to verify (title/publisher/date or URL).

## Rules & cautions
- **Accuracy over completeness.** Do not fabricate figures, statistics, or process details. If exact production numbers or internal procedures are not publicly documented, say so rather than guessing.
- Clearly distinguish "how physical currency is printed and distributed" from "how money is created in the economy" (bank lending, reserves, quantitative easing, etc.) — conflating these is a common misconception this report should actively correct.
- Prefer official/primary sources (central bank and government mint/printer websites) over unverified web content.

## Style
- ~1000-1600 words plus the sources list.
- Neutral, explanatory tone — this is an educational research report, not persuasive writing.
- Use clear section headings matching the structure above.
