# Task: Where the World Market Is Actually Growing — and Which Parts One Person Can Enter

Write the result to `output.md` in this folder.

## Objective
Search wide and deep — market-research and industry data, official statistics, platform and marketplace disclosures, company filings, academic work on self-employment and small-business outcomes, and practitioner sources — and produce a single **decision dossier** that answers two linked questions:

1. **Which parts of the world economy are genuinely growing fast right now**, with real numbers, real sources, and an honest account of which "growth" figures are reliable and which are marketing.
2. **Which of those growing areas a single person can actually enter** — with no employees, no outside investment, and ordinary personal savings — and what the realistic economics of doing so look like, including the failure rates.

The deliverable is a **screened, evidence-tiered map of solo-accessible opportunities**, not a list of business ideas. Every market-size and growth claim must be traceable to a named source with a date and a methodology note. Every income figure must be a distribution, never a single inspiring number.

## Framing — the discipline of this task
This topic is the most contaminated subject on the internet. The entire "make money online / financial freedom" genre exists to sell courses, and its numbers are mostly fabricated or laundered. Five failure modes must be actively resisted:

1. **The fabricated market size.** "The $X billion market growing at Y% CAGR" is very often a press release for a paywalled report whose methodology is unpublished. Prefer official statistics and company filings. Where only a commercial forecast exists, **name the firm, the report, the publication date, and say that the methodology is not public.** Where two reputable firms disagree wildly on the same market, show both figures — the spread is itself the finding.
2. **Survivorship bias as a business model.** Case studies are selected on success. For every opportunity, the report must ask: *how many people tried this and what happened to the median one?* Where a base rate exists (platform payout distributions, self-employment survival data, app-store revenue concentration), cite it. Where none exists, say so.
3. **The course-seller's incentive.** Any source that teaches a money-making method while selling access to that teaching is tier (C) and must be labelled. Note explicitly where the most visible "experts" in a niche make their money from teaching it rather than doing it.
4. **Confusing market growth with personal opportunity.** A market can grow 40% a year while being entirely closed to individuals (semiconductor fabrication, defence, pharmaceuticals, grid-scale storage). Growth and accessibility are two separate axes and the report must score them separately.
5. **Selling an outcome.** "Financial freedom" is the user's stated goal and should be addressed honestly — including what the arithmetic actually requires, how long it plausibly takes, and how often it does not happen. **Do not promise it.**

### Evidence tiers — label every figure
- **(A) Primary/official** — national statistical offices (BLS, Eurostat, ONS, national census bureaus), central banks, OECD/IMF/World Bank/UNCTAD/WTO, SEC filings and earnings calls, platform transparency and creator-economy reports published by the platform itself, app-store and marketplace official statistics, government tax and self-employment data.
- **(B) Independent** — peer-reviewed research, NBER and similar working papers, reputable trade press with its own reporting, established data providers where the methodology is published (e.g. Sensor Tower, Statista with the underlying source named, Stripe/Shopify/Payoneer aggregate data), and audited industry bodies.
- **(C) Reported/promotional** — commercial market-research press releases, vendor blogs, course sellers, YouTube/podcast claims, income-report blog posts, "top 10 side hustles" listicles, and any figure whose original source cannot be traced.

**Rule: no opportunity may be recommended on tier (C) evidence alone.** If the only evidence is (C), the opportunity goes in a clearly-marked "unverified / claimed" annex.

## Scope

### Part 1 — The macro map: where growth actually is (required)
Establish the landscape before narrowing to solo work.
- **Global growth by region and sector** as of the research date — GDP growth by region (IMF/World Bank), and the fastest-growing sectors by revenue, employment, and investment. State the as-of date and the forecast vintage.
- **Structural shifts worth naming**, each with a data point and a source: AI and the compute build-out; the energy transition and electrification; demographic ageing and the care economy; the reshoring/nearshoring of supply chains; the continued shift of commerce, media and services online; emerging-market consumer growth (India, Indonesia, Nigeria, Vietnam, Brazil); healthcare and longevity; defence and security spending; climate adaptation; and the informal/cross-border digital services economy.
- **A "growth vs. accessibility" matrix** — the central analytical device of the report. Plot 20–30 sectors on two axes: *measured growth rate* and *minimum viable capital and credential barrier for an individual*. Most fast-growing sectors will land in the "high growth, closed to individuals" quadrant. **Say so plainly** — that is the honest headline, and it is what separates this report from the genre.
- **Where growth is NOT** — declining or structurally shrinking areas an individual should avoid entering now, with evidence.

### Part 2 — The solo-accessibility screen (required)
Define and apply an explicit filter. An opportunity qualifies as **solo-accessible** only if, as documented:
- **Startup capital under ~$10,000 USD** (state the actual figure per opportunity; note where it is far lower).
- **No employees required** to reach meaningful revenue; contractors and automation permitted.
- **No licence, degree, or regulatory approval** that takes more than ~12 months to obtain — or, where one is required, say exactly what it is, what it costs, and how long it takes.
- **No outside investment required.**
- **Legally open to an individual** in most jurisdictions, with a note on where it is not.

Score every candidate on a consistent rubric, presented as a table:
**Market growth (A/B/C-tiered figure) | Startup capital | Time to first revenue | Skill barrier | Competitive intensity | Durability (is this a 12-month arbitrage or a 10-year business?) | Income ceiling for one person | Automation/AI displacement risk | Evidence tier.**

### Part 3 — The opportunity profiles (required, the core of the report)
Profile **15–20 opportunities** that pass the screen. Cover a genuine range — do not let this collapse into an all-digital list. The set must include, at minimum:
- At least **4 that are not digital** (local services, trades, physical goods, equipment-based, logistics, care).
- At least **3 that are B2B rather than consumer-facing** — B2B is systematically under-covered in this genre and has better unit economics.
- At least **3 viable from a low-income or emerging-market country**, not only from the US/EU.
- At least **2 that primarily monetise an existing professional skill** rather than requiring a new one.
- At least **3 tied to a structural shift identified in Part 1** (AI tooling and integration, energy/electrification services, ageing and care, reshoring, compliance).
- At least **2 with genuinely low competitive intensity** because they are boring, unglamorous, or require doing something tedious well.

Candidate space to investigate — **verify each; several will fail the screen and the failures are part of the deliverable**: AI implementation and automation consulting for small businesses; vertical AI tooling and micro-SaaS; data labelling and evaluation work; prompt/agent engineering services; niche B2B software; API and integration work; technical and regulatory writing; specialised bookkeeping and fractional finance; compliance and certification support (ISO, SOC 2, accessibility, EU AI Act); cybersecurity assessment for SMBs; e-commerce and private label; print-on-demand; dropshipping (**check the actual failure rate — this one is heavily promoted and poorly evidenced**); Amazon FBA; digital products and courses (note the reflexivity: the genre selling this *is* this); paid newsletters and niche media; YouTube and podcasting (**cite the actual payout distribution, not the top-creator figures**); Substack/Patreon economics; freelance marketplaces and their rate distributions; specialised recruiting; solar and EV-charger installation and adjacent services; HVAC/electrical/plumbing trades and the retirement-driven supply gap; home care and elder services; pet services; self-storage and small-scale rental arbitrage; vending and micro-retail; equipment rental; mobile services (detailing, repair); short-term rental management as a service; local SEO and marketing services; translation and localisation post-AI (**check whether AI has destroyed or grown this**); specialty food and beverage; secondhand/resale and reCommerce; collectibles and platform arbitrage (cross-reference `tasks/26-whatnot-platform-research/`); remote roles arbitraged across cost-of-living differences; cross-border services from low-cost jurisdictions.

**Each profile must contain:**
- **What it actually is** — in two or three concrete sentences, described as the work, not the dream.
- **The market evidence** — size and growth with source, date, and tier. If the number is weak, say the number is weak.
- **The economics** — realistic startup capital, fixed and variable costs, pricing, gross margin, time to first revenue, and time to a living income. Show the arithmetic.
- **The income distribution, not the average** — median and the shape of the tail wherever data exists (platform payout reports, BLS self-employment earnings, marketplace disclosures). **Where no distribution exists, write "no reliable distribution data" — do not substitute an anecdote.**
- **What actually kills it** — the specific, common failure mode, and roughly what proportion of entrants hit it, with a source where one exists.
- **Competition and moat** — why this is or isn't already saturated; what a solo operator can defend.
- **AI exposure** — is AI a tailwind, a headwind, or an extinction event for this work over 3–5 years? Argue it.
- **Skills and time to competence** — what you must be able to do, and roughly how long it takes to get there from zero.
- **Legal, tax and jurisdictional notes** — licensing, platform terms, tax treatment of self-employment, and the obvious pitfalls. General, not advice.
- **First 90 days** — a concrete, testable sequence, ending in a **cheap falsifiable test**: the smallest experiment that would tell you within weeks whether to continue, and what result should make you stop.
- **Verdict** — a one-line honest judgement, including "not recommended" where that is the answer.

### Part 4 — The honest arithmetic of "financial freedom" (required)
Treat the user's actual goal seriously and quantitatively.
- **Define the terms** — replacement income, a cash-flow-covering asset base, and the difference between a job you own and an asset that pays you without your time.
- **The actual maths** — the withdrawal-rate literature (Bengen 1994, the Trinity study and its later critiques, Vanguard/Morningstar updates), what capital is needed for a given income at various assumed rates, and how that varies by country and cost of living. Present as a table. **Name the assumptions and the critiques; do not present 4% as settled.**
- **Two routes compared** — building a business that sells (equity event) versus building cash flow and investing the surplus. Cover the realities of small-business valuation multiples and how often solo businesses actually sell.
- **Time-to-goal scenarios** — a table showing plausible timelines under conservative, moderate, and optimistic savings-rate and return assumptions. Label every assumption. **Show the downside scenarios with equal prominence.**
- **Base rates** — new-business survival rates (BLS Business Employment Dynamics or the national equivalent), self-employment income distributions versus employment, and the share of solo ventures that reach a full-time income. This section is the antidote to the rest of the report; give it real weight.
- **The risk column** — what is given up: income stability, benefits and healthcare, pension contributions, career capital, and the psychological cost. Include the opportunity cost of not taking a salaried job in a growing field, which is frequently the better risk-adjusted choice and is almost never mentioned in this genre.

### Part 5 — Method, tools and what to avoid (recommended additions)
- **How to size a market yourself** — a short, practical method: from official statistics to bottom-up estimation, and the questions that expose a fake number.
- **How to validate demand before building** — concrete cheap tests (pre-sales, landing-page tests, manual delivery before automation, cold outreach with a real price), and what a genuine signal looks like versus a vanity one.
- **Free and cheap data sources** — a table: World Bank Open Data, IMF WEO, OECD.Stat, UN Comtrade, Eurostat, BLS/Census, Google Trends, app-store and marketplace rankings, Crunchbase, job postings as a demand indicator, and platform transparency reports. Say what each is genuinely good for.
- **The scam and near-scam taxonomy** — MLM, "passive income" course funnels, signal groups and copy-trading, forex and crypto "education", dropshipping-course economics, franchise-fee traps, and AI-generated content farms. For each: the tell, and the regulator or consumer-protection resource that documents it (FTC, ASA, national equivalents). This protects the reader more than any opportunity in the report.
- **Reading list** — 8–12 genuinely useful books and papers with author, publisher, year, and one line on *what it teaches and where it is unreliable*. Include at least two that are sceptical of the entrepreneurship narrative.
- **Cross-references** — `tasks/17-self-made-billionaires/`, `tasks/28-wealth-from-nothing-personal-stories/` (the biographical counterpart: what actually worked historically), `tasks/27-startup-building-speechify-like-app/` (a worked product example), `tasks/25-youtube-platform-research/` and `tasks/26-whatnot-platform-research/` (two specific platform economies). **Do not restate what those folders already cover** — reference them by path and add only what is new here.

## Required structure of `output.md`
1. **Title + scope line** — coverage, the date the research was performed, currency and base year for all figures, and a note that market forecasts are estimates.
2. **Method & evidence tiers** — the (A)/(B)/(C) convention and the solo-accessibility screen, compactly.
3. **TL;DR** — 10–14 bullets, each with its evidence tier. At least three of them must be things the reader should *not* do.
4. **The macro map** — global growth, structural shifts, and the growth-vs-accessibility matrix.
5. **The screen** — criteria, and the table of what passed and what failed, with the reason for each failure.
6. **Master comparison table** — all 15–20 opportunities scored on the Part 2 rubric, sortable by reading.
7. **Opportunity profiles** — 15–20, each following the Part 3 structure.
8. **The honest arithmetic of financial freedom** — Part 4, including the base-rate section.
9. **Method, tools and validation** — how to do this research yourself.
10. **Scams and near-scams** — the taxonomy.
11. **Unverified / claimed annex** — opportunities where only tier (C) evidence exists, clearly quarantined.
12. **Open questions / what could not be verified** — explicit list.
13. **Reading list.**
14. **Sources** — every source with author/title, publisher, URL, publication or access date, and evidence tier.

## Rules & cautions
- **Accuracy over completeness.** Do not fabricate market sizes, CAGRs, income figures, survival rates, company names, report titles, or study findings. A shorter verified report beats a padded one.
- **Never invent a number to fill a table cell.** Write "not documented" and carry it to the open-questions list.
- **Every market figure needs a named source, a date, and a methodology note.** "The market is projected to reach $X by 20YY" is not a usable claim without the firm and report named.
- **Income claims must be distributions.** Where you can only find a top-end or promotional figure, label it and state that it is not representative.
- **No income promises, no guarantees, no timelines presented as expectations.** State plainly and early that most solo ventures do not reach a full-time income, and cite the base rate.
- **Do not recommend anything requiring recruitment of other participants to profit** (MLM and its variants), and name that structure where it appears.
- **Do not recommend leveraged trading, forex, crypto speculation, or signal-following as a path to income**, and say why the evidence on retail outcomes is what it is, with sources (e.g. broker-disclosed retail loss rates under regulatory requirement).
- **Be explicit about jurisdiction.** Tax treatment, licensing, platform eligibility, and payment-rail access differ enormously by country; note where an opportunity is US/EU-only.
- **Do not sanitise the downside.** Saturation, platform dependency and de-platforming risk, algorithm changes, AI displacement, and burnout are part of every honest profile.
- **This is educational research, not financial, tax, legal, or investment advice.** State this clearly. Recommend consulting a qualified professional for tax and legal structure in the reader's own jurisdiction.
- If web access is unavailable or a claim cannot be verified, say so explicitly and list what was attempted — an honest gap is a correct output.

## Style
- ~5,000–7,000 words plus tables, matrix, and sources.
- **Plain, sceptical, practical tone** — written for one specific reader deciding how to spend the next two years of their life. No hype, no motivational register, no second-person cheerleading. Equally, no reflexive cynicism: where something genuinely works, say so and show the evidence.
- Lead with numbers; use tables wherever the material is comparative (macro growth, the matrix, the screen, the master comparison, the freedom arithmetic, data sources, scam taxonomy, reading list).
- Mark uncertain figures inline with their tier — e.g. "~$4.2bn in 2025 (C, vendor press release; methodology not public)".
- Where an opportunity is bad, say it is bad and why. A report that recommends nothing in a section is a legitimate outcome.
- Use clear section headings matching the structure above, and cross-reference sibling folders by path.
