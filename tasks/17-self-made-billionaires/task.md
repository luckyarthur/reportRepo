# Task: Deep Research — How People Became Billionaires (Historical Study)

Write the result to `output.md` in this folder.

## Objective
Research the historical record of people who built extraordinary fortunes — from the industrial-age tycoons through today's technology and investing billionaires — and identify **how** they actually did it. The point is not to produce a rich list, but to reverse-engineer the repeatable mechanics and conditions behind large fortunes, and to be honest about the role of timing, inheritance, and luck. This is a research task, not motivational writing — every factual claim about people, companies, dates, or figures must be accurate and, where possible, sourced.

## Scope
Cover a spread across eras and geographies (do not make this a list of only modern American tech founders). At minimum, include representative figures from:
- **Industrial age (roughly 1860–1930)** — e.g., John D. Rockefeller (oil), Andrew Carnegie (steel), Cornelius Vanderbilt (railroads/shipping), Henry Ford (automobiles). Note where wealth estimates are inflation-adjusted or contested.
- **Post-war / consumer era (roughly 1945–1990)** — e.g., Sam Walton (retail), Warren Buffett (investing), Ingvar Kamprad (IKEA), Soichiro Honda or another non-Western example.
- **Technology era (roughly 1975–2010)** — e.g., Bill Gates, Steve Jobs, Larry Page/Sergey Brin, Jeff Bezos.
- **Contemporary (2000–present)** — e.g., Elon Musk, Zhang Yiming (ByteDance), Bernard Arnault (luxury conglomerate), Jensen Huang (NVIDIA), plus at least one non-US, non-tech example.

For each figure profiled, cover: starting circumstances (family wealth/education/capital access), the specific vehicle of wealth (what asset actually appreciated), the pivotal decision or bet, and roughly when/how the fortune compounded.

## Required structure of `output.md`
1. **Title + scope line** stating the coverage (eras and regions) and the date the research was performed.
2. **TL;DR** — 5-7 bullet points on the biggest recurring findings.
3. **Comparison table** — a table of profiled individuals (Name | Era | Country | Primary source of wealth | Starting circumstances | Key mechanism).
4. **Profiles** — 10-15 individuals, each a short write-up (roughly 100-200 words) following the per-figure coverage listed under Scope above.
5. **Recurring patterns** — the analytical core. Identify the common mechanics that show up repeatedly, such as:
   - **Equity ownership, not salary** — nearly all large fortunes are concentrated ownership of an appreciating asset, not high income.
   - **Timing into a structural shift** — oil, rail, semiconductors, internet, mobile, AI; being early to a platform shift.
   - **Scale economics / network effects / moats** — why some businesses compound and most do not.
   - **Reinvestment and compounding over decades** — time horizon as a variable.
   - **Access to capital and credit** — and who historically had that access.
   - **Concentration vs. diversification** — fortunes are usually made concentrated, kept diversified.
6. **The uncomfortable variables** — an explicitly honest section on inheritance and starting capital (what fraction of billionaires are genuinely self-made vs. inherited — cite a study if available), survivorship bias (the same risk-taking that produced winners produced far more failures), luck and timing, and cases where fortunes involved monopoly power, regulatory capture, or practices later ruled illegal (e.g., Standard Oil's antitrust breakup). Do not sanitize this.
7. **What actually generalizes** — a grounded closing section separating the transferable lessons (ownership, compounding, positioning into structural change, long time horizons) from the non-transferable ones (being born in the right decade with the right access). Avoid survivorship-bias-driven advice.
8. **Sources** — consolidated list of references (biographies, academic studies on wealth mobility, Forbes/Bloomberg wealth data, reputable historical sources), each with enough detail to verify (title/publisher/date or URL).

## Rules & cautions
- **Accuracy over completeness.** Do not fabricate net-worth figures, dates, quotes, or anecdotes. Wealth estimates are inherently imprecise — present them as estimates and name the source/date of the estimate.
- Historical net worths must be clearly labeled as either nominal (at the time) or inflation-adjusted, and note that inflation-adjusted historical fortunes are contested methodologically.
- Widely repeated founder anecdotes are often embellished or apocryphal. Flag any story that is popular but not well documented rather than repeating it as fact.
- **Do not write this as motivational content.** The repo already contains *Think and Grow Rich* success narratives; this task is the analytical, evidence-based counterpart to those, and should note where the popular narrative diverges from documented history.
- Prefer reputable sources (biographies, academic research on wealth and mobility, established financial data providers) over unverified web content.

## Style
- ~1500-2200 words plus the comparison table and sources list.
- Neutral, analytical tone.
- Use clear section headings matching the structure above.
