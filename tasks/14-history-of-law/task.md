# Task: Deep Research — The History of the Creation of Law

Write the result to `output.md` in this folder.

## Objective
Produce a deep-research report on the history of law as a human institution: where and when the first laws/legal codes emerged, why they were created (the original purposes), how law evolved over time across civilizations, and the pivotal events and people who shaped that evolution. This is a research task, not an opinion piece — every factual claim about dates, codes, rulers, or events must be accurate and, where possible, sourced.

## Scope
Cover the full arc from the earliest known legal codes through to the emergence of modern legal systems. At minimum, address:
- **Origins** — the earliest known law codes and legal traditions (e.g., Code of Ur-Nammu, Code of Hammurabi, Hittite laws, Mosaic Law/Torah, early Chinese legalism), roughly what they regulated, and why they were created at first (social order, dispute resolution, protecting property/trade, consolidating royal/religious authority, standardizing punishment, etc.).
- **Classical antiquity** — Greek law (e.g., Draco, Solon, Athenian democracy's legal innovations) and Roman law (Twelve Tables through Justinian's Corpus Juris Civilis), and their influence on later systems.
- **Medieval developments** — canon law, feudal law, the emergence of common law in England (including Magna Carta, 1215), Islamic law (Sharia) codification, and other regional traditions relevant to the topic.
- **Codification era** — the rise of modern civil law codes (e.g., the Napoleonic Code, 1804) and how codification reshaped continental legal systems; the parallel development of common law through case precedent.
- **Modern era** — the spread of constitutional law, international law, human rights law (e.g., UN Declaration of Human Rights, 1948), and the divergence/convergence of common law vs. civil law traditions in the world today.

## Required structure of `output.md`
1. **Title + scope line** stating the coverage (origins through modern era) and the date the research was performed.
2. **TL;DR** — 4–6 bullet points on the biggest turning points in legal history.
3. **Timeline** — a table of major legal milestones (Year/Era | Event or Code | Civilization/Region | Why it mattered).
4. **Origins and original purpose** — detailed section on the earliest law codes and the specific problems they were created to solve.
5. **Evolution through the ages** — organized chronologically (Ancient → Classical → Medieval → Codification era → Modern), explaining how and why law changed at each stage.
6. **Key events** — dedicated write-ups (2-5 sentences each) for the most significant events (e.g., Code of Hammurabi's promulgation, the Twelve Tables, Magna Carta, the Napoleonic Code, the UN Declaration of Human Rights).
7. **Key people** — dedicated write-ups (2-5 sentences each) for the most significant figures (e.g., Hammurabi, Solon, Justinian I, King John/the barons at Runnymede, Napoleon Bonaparte, William Blackstone), covering what they did and their lasting influence on law.
8. **Common law vs. civil law today** — short section explaining how these two dominant modern traditions trace back to the historical threads above.
9. **Sources** — consolidated list of references used (books, academic sources, or reputable web sources), each with enough detail to verify (title/publisher/date or URL).

## Rules & cautions
- **Accuracy over completeness.** Do not fabricate codes, dates, quotes, or people. If a specific date or detail is disputed or uncertain among historians, say so explicitly rather than presenting it as settled fact.
- Distinguish clearly between well-established historical consensus and more speculative/contested claims (e.g., exact dating of very early codes).
- Prefer reputable sources (academic/historical references, established encyclopedic or legal-history sources) over unverified web content.

## Style
- ~1200-1800 words plus the timeline table and sources list.
- Neutral, analytical tone — this is a historical research report, not persuasive writing.
- Use clear section headings matching the structure above.
