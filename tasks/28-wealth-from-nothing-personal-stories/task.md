# Task: Built From Nothing — Personal Stories of Wealth Created Without Starting Capital (World Book-Library Research)

Write the result to `output.md` in this folder.

## Objective
Search the world's book literature — autobiographies, memoirs, authorised and independent biographies, oral-history collections, company histories, and academic work on wealth and mobility — together with other verifiable sources, and produce a single **study dossier** on people who genuinely built substantial wealth **starting from nothing**.

The report must answer three things for every person profiled:
1. **Where they actually started** — family circumstances, capital, schooling, and access, stated concretely rather than romantically.
2. **The dated track of their life** — a real chronological timeline from birth to fortune (and, where relevant, to loss), not a highlights reel.
3. **How the wealth was actually built** — the specific asset that appreciated, the mechanism, the pivotal decisions, the capital they used and where it came from.

Plus a fourth thing for the reader: **what is genuinely studiable here**, including a structured reading list and a method for reading rags-to-riches stories critically.

This is an evidence-grounded research task, not motivational writing. Every factual claim about a person, company, date, or figure must be accurate and sourced.

## Relationship to the existing folders — read this first
- `tasks/01`–`tasks/11` retell *Think and Grow Rich* narratives (Barnes, Darby, Ford, Edison, Carnegie, Schwab, Candler, Gunsaulus, Wier, Hill, James J. Hill). Those are **motivational retellings of a single 1937 source**.
- `tasks/17-self-made-billionaires/` is the analytical counterpart: how large fortunes were built across eras, **including people who started wealthy**.

This task is **narrower and stricter than 17**: the entry requirement is a genuine from-nothing start, and the deliverable is a *personal-story dossier with life timelines plus a study curriculum*, not a mechanics survey. Where a figure already appears in another folder (Carnegie, Ford, Rockefeller, Walton), keep the treatment here short, cross-reference the folder by path, and **do not restate what is already written there** — add only the from-nothing evidence, the timeline, and the reading.

## Framing — the discipline of this task
Rags-to-riches is the single most contaminated genre in business writing. Three specific failure modes must be actively resisted:

1. **The laundered origin story.** Founders and their publicists systematically understate starting advantages. "Started in a garage" frequently means "started in a garage owned by a professional-class family, with a university degree and a network." Apply the from-nothing filter to *documented* facts, not to the subject's own framing.
2. **The apocryphal anecdote.** The best-known story about a wealthy person is very often the least verifiable one. Where a famous anecdote traces only to the subject's own later retelling or to an uncited secondary source, **say so explicitly** rather than repeating it as fact.
3. **Survivorship bias by construction.** Selecting on outcome and inferring method is the structural error of the entire genre. The report must carry a section that takes this seriously, with base rates.

### The "from nothing" filter — define and apply it explicitly
State the criteria in the output and apply them consistently. A person qualifies if **all** of the following hold, as documented:
- **No meaningful inherited or family capital** at the point the wealth-building began (a small family loan is disqualifying only if it was large relative to the venture — say which, with the figure).
- **No family business, seat, or title to step into.**
- **Starting circumstances at or below their society's median** — poverty, refugee or migrant status, orphanhood, manual labour, or comparable.
- **The fortune was built in their own lifetime, by them**, not inherited or married into.

Explicitly mark near-misses as **"partial"** rather than silently including or excluding them (e.g., people from modest but stable professional families, or with a degree from an elite institution that supplied the network). Ambiguity is the interesting part — surface it.

### Evidence tiers — label every figure
- **(A) Primary/official** — company filings, court records, census and immigration records, official pricing, contemporaneous newspaper reports, the subject's own book where the claim is about their own life *and* is checkable.
- **(B) Independent** — scholarly biography, peer-reviewed research, reputable business press with its own sourcing, established wealth-data providers (Forbes, Bloomberg, Hurun) with the list and date named.
- **(C) Reported/promotional** — authorised biography, subject interviews, corporate "our story" pages, motivational literature, celebrity-net-worth aggregators, SEO listicles.

**Treat net-worth figures as estimates always**, naming the provider and the list date. Treat celebrity-net-worth aggregator sites as tier (C) and prefer not to use them at all. Where a widely-repeated origin claim exists only at tier (C), label it and say what would be needed to confirm it.

## Scope

### Coverage requirements
Profile **15–20 individuals**. The set must include, at minimum:
- At least **6 non-Western** figures (Asia, Africa, Latin America, Middle East).
- At least **4 women**.
- At least **3 pre-1900 births** and at least **4 people whose fortune was made after 2000**.
- At least **4 outside technology and finance** (manufacturing, retail, consumer goods, food, media, entertainment, resources, services).
- At least **2 whose starting point was refugee status, statelessness, or forced migration**.
- At least **1 whose wealth was later lost, or whose fortune ended in fraud, collapse, or prosecution** — the genre's dishonesty lies mostly in what it omits.

### Per-profile requirements
Each profile must contain, under clear sub-headings:
- **Starting conditions** — family, income level, schooling, capital, citizenship/immigration status, and any documented discrimination or legal barrier. With tier labels.
- **Life track** — a dated chronological bullet timeline (birth → first work → the pivot → first capital → inflection → scale → present/death, plus any collapse). Minimum ~8 dated entries. Mark approximate dates as "c." rather than inventing precision.
- **How the wealth was built** — the actual asset that appreciated; where the first capital came from and how much it was; the specific mechanism (manufacturing cost advantage, distribution, licensing, brand, network effects, leverage, land, timing into a structural shift); when it compounded.
- **The pivotal decision** — the one or two decisions that most plausibly changed the outcome, argued from evidence.
- **Documented vs. legend** — at least one line naming which parts of the popular story are well documented and which are not.
- **Read this** — the best book(s) on this person, with author, publisher, year, and a one-line note on the book's reliability (autobiography, authorised, independent, hostile).
- **What to study here** — 2–3 specific, transferable observations. Not platitudes.

### Analytical sections required beyond the profiles
- **Cross-cutting patterns** — the mechanics that recur across genuinely from-nothing fortunes specifically: proximity to a growing market rather than a rich one; converting cheap labour or attention into an owned asset; the first-capital problem and how each solved it (savings, customer prepayment, supplier credit, informal lending, a spouse, a partner, sweat equity); geographic or sectoral arbitrage; the role of migration; timing into a structural shift; and the point at which each stopped selling their time.
- **The first-capital table** — the single most useful table in the report: for each person, the amount of their first business capital, in nominal terms with year, its source, and what it bought.
- **The counter-evidence section** — take this seriously and give it real weight:
  - Base rates of mobility from the bottom (cite named research — e.g. Chetty et al. on intergenerational mobility; Kaplan & Rauh on the changing composition of the Forbes 400 — with study titles and dates).
  - What proportion of large fortunes actually begin from nothing, per available research.
  - Survivorship bias stated concretely: the same behaviours produced far more failures, and the failures did not write memoirs.
  - Era-specific conditions that are no longer available (post-war industrial expansion, China's opening after 1978, early internet land-grabs, cheap urban land).
  - Cases where the fortune involved conduct later judged illegal or indefensible, and cases of outright fraud.
- **What transfers and what does not** — separate the genuinely portable lessons from the ones that depend on being born in a particular decade in a particular country.
- **The study curriculum** — the reader-facing payoff:
  - A **tiered reading list**: (i) autobiographies and memoirs, (ii) independent biographies, (iii) academic and statistical work on wealth and mobility, (iv) mechanism books (business, capital, compounding) — each entry with author, title, publisher, year, and one line on *what specifically it teaches and where it is unreliable*.
  - Note which titles are in the **public domain** and where they can be read legally free (Project Gutenberg, Internet Archive, national library digitisations), with links.
  - A **"how to read a rags-to-riches book" checklist** — 8–12 questions to ask of any such account (Who funded the first year? What was the family's actual income? What did the network provide? What is the base rate for this move? Who else did the same thing and failed? What is the source for this anecdote? etc.).
  - A **structured 12-month self-study plan** — what to read in what order and what to extract from each stage.

## Required structure of `output.md`
1. **Title + scope line** — coverage (eras, regions, number of profiles), the date the research was performed, and a note that all wealth figures are point-in-time estimates.
2. **Method & evidence tiers** — the (A)/(B)/(C) convention and the from-nothing filter, stated in one compact section.
3. **TL;DR** — 8–12 bullets, each carrying its finding and evidence tier.
4. **Who qualified, who didn't** — the selection table, including the rejected candidates and the reason each was rejected.
5. **Master comparison table** — Name | Life dates | Country | Starting condition | First paid work | Wealth vehicle | First capital (amount + year) | Peak wealth estimate (source + date) | Best book.
6. **The first-capital table** — as specified above.
7. **Profiles** — 15–20, each following the per-profile structure.
8. **Cross-cutting patterns.**
9. **Counter-evidence: base rates, survivorship, and the fortunes that ended badly.**
10. **What transfers and what does not.**
11. **The study curriculum** — tiered reading list, public-domain notes, the critical-reading checklist, the 12-month plan.
12. **Open questions / what could not be verified** — explicit list.
13. **Sources** — every source with author/title, publisher, URL where applicable, publication or access date, and evidence tier.

## Rules & cautions
- **Accuracy over completeness.** Do not fabricate dates, figures, book titles, publishers, ISBNs, quotes, or anecdotes. A profile with fewer verified facts is better than one padded with plausible invention.
- **Never invent a book.** Every title in the reading list must be a real, published work with a correct author and publisher. If unsure of the publisher or year, say so rather than guessing.
- **Never invent a number to fill a table cell.** Write "not documented" and carry it into the open-questions list.
- **Wealth figures** must name the provider and list date (e.g. "Forbes list, March 2026"), and historical fortunes must be labelled nominal or inflation-adjusted, with a note that inflation-adjusted historical fortunes are methodologically contested.
- **Do not sanitise.** Where a fortune involved monopoly practices, labour violence, political favour, sanctions-era opportunity, or later criminal conviction, state it in the profile.
- **Do not moralise either.** Report conduct; do not editorialise about the person's character.
- **No income or outcome promises.** State plainly that these are outliers selected on success, and that the base rate for replicating any of them is very low.
- **Handle living people carefully.** Stick to documented public facts about their business lives; avoid unverified claims about family, health, or private conduct.
- Prefer scholarly biography and primary records over motivational literature. Where the only source for a claim is *Think and Grow Rich* or a similar work, label it tier (C) and note the folder that already covers it.
- If web access is unavailable or a claim cannot be verified, say so explicitly and list what was attempted — an honest gap is a correct output.
- This is educational and historical research, not financial, legal, or investment advice; include a brief note to that effect.

## Style
- ~4,000–6,000 words plus tables and the sources list.
- Neutral, analytical, biographical tone — written for someone who intends to actually read the books afterwards.
- Use tables wherever the data is comparative (selection, master comparison, first capital, reading list).
- Mark uncertain figures inline with their tier — e.g. "left school at 15 (B, scholarly biography)" — rather than relegating all sourcing to the endnotes.
- Use clear section headings matching the structure above.
- Cross-reference sibling folders by path (e.g. `tasks/05-andrew-carnegie/`, `tasks/17-self-made-billionaires/`) wherever they overlap.
